# Assembly Guide — 10 ft Hex Cedar Gazebo
**Project:** Saanich BC | Traditional mortise & tenon | No metal hangers  
**Sequence:** 4-phase site build | All compound angles pre-cut in shop

---

## Before You Start

### Required Tools on Site
- Framing square + 48" level + string lines
- Cordless drill + 13/16" auger bit (drawbore holes)
- Rubber mallet (pegs) + 3 lb hammer
- One 6 ft step ladder + one 8 ft ladder
- Bar clamps × 4 (beam ring glue-up)
- Chalk line + batter boards
- Eye protection + hearing protection

### Crew Size
- **Phase 1–2:** 1 person adequate.  
- **Phase 3 (beam ring + post tops):** minimum **2 people** — one on the post, one below holding level.  
- **Phase 4 (rafters + hub):** minimum **2 people**; a third helping hand makes hub insertion much easier.

### Weather Window
Glued joints need +10 °C and no rain for 24 h. Do not assemble in rain — cedar absorbs moisture and joinery will swell, making final fitting difficult.

---

## Phase 1 — Site Prep and Caissons

**Goal:** 6 concrete caissons at exact hex layout, J-bolts set true.

### 1.1 — Lay out hex centre and post positions
1. Drive a centre stake.
2. Set batter boards at minimum 3 ft outside the structure on four sides.
3. From centre, mark 6 post positions at exactly **5'-0" radius** (10'-0" point-to-point diameter) and **60° intervals**.
4. Verify with diagonal check: any two posts 120° apart should be exactly 8'-8" apart. If not, re-measure.

### 1.2 — Dig caissons
- Diameter: **12"**
- Depth: **24" minimum** (Saanich frost depth 18"; 6" of bearing below)
- Excavate with hand augur or rented power augur.
- Remove all loose material from bottom.

### 1.3 — Set sonotubes and pour
1. Drop sonotube into each hole; top should extend 2" above grade.
2. Mix 2 × 80 lb bags Quikrete per caisson (stiff mix, not soupy).
3. Pour and rod out air pockets.
4. **Immediately** set J-bolt at exact centre, projecting 3.5" above top of tube.
5. Check bolt is plumb with a level held against the shank.
6. Let cure minimum **72 hours** before placing any load.

### Phase 1 Checkpoint
- [x] All 6 bolt centres within ±1/8" of hex layout
- [x] All bolts plumb
- [x] Tops of pier blocks level to each other within 1/4" (use water level or laser)

## Phase 2 — Post Preparation (shop phase, before site)

**Goal:** All posts finished and mortised; compound cuts confirmed.

### 2.1 — Saw setup for compound cuts (CRITICAL — do on scrap first)
```
Compound cut settings for all hip rafter seat cuts:
  Saw miter angle: 28.71°  (rotate table/fence)
  Saw bevel angle:  9.10°  (tilt blade)
  Reference: geometry_engine.py output — see context/staging/structural-model.json
```
1. Mark a sacrificial 4×6 scrap identically to a real rafter: crown side, run direction, hub end vs tail end.
2. Make the compound cut.
3. Test the scrap against an actual beam face. The cut shoulder should sit flat against the beam top with no rocking and no gap.
4. Only then proceed with real stock.

### 2.2 — Cut and mark posts
1. Cut all 6 posts to **8'-4" (100")** plumb-square both ends.
2. Mark each post: **bottom** (J-bolt end), **outward face**, and **beam face** position at 8'-4" from bottom.
3. Seal ALL end grain with CPES epoxy consolidant — let cure before any glue-up.

### 2.3 — Post mortises
- Mark beam-face mortise position: top of post, beam face side.
- Mortise dimensions: 2.125" × 4.125" × 3" deep (blind mortise).
- Use 1.5" bench chisel and mallet; clean to flat bottom.
- Mark brace mortise positions 18" down from top on two adjacent faces.

### 2.4 — Apply finish
Apply two coats Penofin Cedar Transparent to all surfaces **including inside mortise pockets**. Let dry fully before assembly.

---

## Phase 3 — Erect Posts and Beam Ring

**Goal:** 6 posts plumb and locked; beam ring installed and pegged.

### 3.1 — Place post bases and set posts
1. Thread post standoff bases (Simpson E66) onto J-bolts; set nut and washer hand-tight only.
2. Set posts in bases; plumb each with a level on two adjacent faces.
3. Brace with diagonal 2×4 kickers to stakes in the ground (minimum 2 kickers per post at 90°).
4. Once all 6 posts are plumb, torque J-bolt nuts to spec.

### 3.2 — Assemble beam ring
The beam ring is 6 × 4×8 cedar, mitered at 28.71° opposing ends.  
**Dry-fit on the ground first — the ring must close with no gaps before it goes up.**

1. Lay all 6 beams on flat ground in a hex ring.
2. Install tenons-into-post mortises with a firm mallet tap — no glue yet.
3. Check diagonals for square.
4. Once fit confirmed: disassemble, apply Titebond III to tenon shoulders only (not inside mortise), re-assemble.
5. Drive 2 × 3/4" oak drawbore pegs per joint. Pegs should drive snug with a mallet; do not overdrive.

### 3.3 — Raise beam ring to post tops
1. With two people: lift one end of ring to post top, seat the tenon.
2. Go around the ring: seat each tenon before pegging any.
3. When all 6 tenons are seated, check the beam ring is level (±1/4" acceptable).
4. Peg all 6 post-to-beam joints with 2 × 3/4" oak pegs.

### Phase 3 Checkpoint
- [x] All posts plumb to <1/4" off-plumb in 8 ft
- [x] Beam ring level to ±1/4"
- [x] All miter joints tight — no visible gaps from 5 ft
- [x] All pegs driven flush or up to 1/8" proud (sanded flush before finish)

---

## Phase 4 — Knee Braces, Rafters, Hub, and Purlins

### 4.1 — King post hub preparation
1. All 6 rafter mortises should already be cut in the hub (see cut list M04).
2. Number each mortise and its corresponding rafter — they are not interchangeable once fitted.
3. Hub sits at top of king-post assembly; will be raised after all rafters are ready.

### 4.2 — Install knee braces
1. Fit all 12 knee braces (4×4, 36", 45° compound bevel both ends) now — before rafters obstruct access.
2. Brace tenons enter matching mortises on post and beam face.
3. Drive 2 × 3/4" oak pegs per brace.
4. Braces should be tight and non-rocking when done.

### 4.3 — Install hip rafters and hub

> This is the most challenging step — rehearse order with crew.

**Option A (scaffolding available):**
1. Install hub on temporary centring jig at apex height.
2. Feed rafters one by one, seating bird's mouth on beam top and tenon into hub mortise.
3. Once all 6 rafters are hand-seated, tap home and peg.

**Option B (ground pre-assembly):**
1. Assemble all 6 rafters into hub on the ground as a complete wheel.
2. Lift entire assembly as a unit onto the beam ring (needs 3+ people and a ladder).
3. Seat all 6 bird's mouths simultaneously; adjust until each sits flat.
4. Temporarily brace with diagonal 1×3 strips while pegging.

**Either method:** Peg each rafter-to-hub tenon with 2 × 3/4" oak pegs before removing any temporary bracing.

### 4.4 — Install purlin ring
1. Purlins (4×4, 3'-3") run between rafters mid-span.
2. Half-lap over each rafter — mark and cut in place.
3. Glue and peg with 1 × 3/4" oak peg per joint.
4. Purlin ring adds lateral rigidity — do not omit.

### Phase 4 Checkpoint
- [x] All knee braces installed with pegs
- [x] All 6 rafters seated at beam (bird's mouth fully bearing) and at hub
- [x] Hub plumb (hangs directly over centre stake)
- [x] Purlin ring installed and pegged

---

## Phase 5 — Final Finish

1. Remove all temporary kicker bracing.
2. Touch-up all exposed end grain with CPES.
3. Apply third coat Penofin to any areas disturbed during assembly.
4. Inspect all drawbore pegs — sand proud pegs flush.
5. Final plumb check of all posts; verify no beam sag at midspan.
6. Photograph finished structure from all 6 faces (one per 60°).

## Compound Cut Quick Reference

| Cut | Miter | Bevel | At which end |
|-----|-------|-------|-------------|
| Hip rafter top (seat) | 28.71° | 9.10° | Hub/top end |
| Hip rafter tail | 0° | 9.10° | Tail/overhang end |
| Ring beam both ends | 28.71° | 0° | Both ends (opposing) |
| Knee brace both ends | 45° | 0° | Both ends |
| Purlin half-lap | — | 9.10° | Field fit |

> Source: `context/staging/structural-model.json` — do not re-derive angles from scratch. These were computed by `geometry_engine.py`. Test on scrap before every production cut run.
